Swift is safe, expressive, and performance-focused, but there’s another modern programming language with many of the same goals: Rust. Developers increasingly benefit from working across languages and platforms — and while Swift can be used for servers, command-line tools, WebAssembly and embedded systems, Rust is far more established in these areas.

I’ve been using Swift since its release in 2014 and recently thoroughly enjoyed learning Rust.

This talk introduces Rust for an iOS developer. We’ll look at tooling, package management, strong typing, expressive enums, and safety-first design, focusing on what feels familiar from Swift. We’ll see how the two languages take different approaches to memory management (which are both unusual).

You’ll leave with a practical mental model of Rust and new ideas you can apply to your Swift development, even if you never write Rust professionally.

## Douglas Hill

Douglas spent many years as the iOS team lead at Nutrient (previously known as PSPDFKit) and recently switched to work across a wider range of technologies at the company. He’s a long-time organiser of the NSLondon iOS developer community.
